                                              Abstract

      Acute stroke, intracranial hemorrhage, and traumatic brain injury require prompt assess-
   ment because delays in recognizing critical findings can affect treatment decisions and patient
   outcomes. However, timely interpretation of brain imaging may be difficult in resource-
   constrained settings where radiology expertise, specialist coverage, and diagnostic infras-
   tructure are limited. This article presents the design of a computer-vision triage framework
   for brain computed tomography (CT) and magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), bringing data
   science and analytics together with clinical medicine and surgery. Its objective is to support
   the prioritization of stroke, hemorrhage, mass effect, and apparently normal studies without
   replacing professional diagnosis. The workflow accepts CT images, MRI images, or both
   CT and MRI images within a single study, while retaining the modality assigned to each
   image. It applies documented image validation, modality-aware preprocessing, and quantita-
   tive feature extraction, and defines a comparative modeling plan for feature-based machine
   learning and convolutional-neural-network deep learning. The application is designed to
   combine image-derived screening impressions with clinician-entered presenting history and
   examination information, then present structured findings, confidence, priority, and differ-
   ential considerations for qualified review. Evaluation is specified using patient-level data
   partitions and clinically relevant measures of discrimination, sensitivity, specificity, F1-score,
   area under the receiver-operating-characteristic curve, calibration, subgroup performance,
   and robustness across modalities, sites, scanners, and acquisition conditions. The framework
   also incorporates explainability, privacy safeguards, data governance, human oversight, and
   implementation considerations for African and other settings with constrained resources.
   The current application is a research prototype, and no clinical performance or outcome
   benefit is claimed. Such claims require representative, appropriately annotated datasets,
   external validation, and prospective workflow evaluation. This work therefore contributes a
   reproducible design and validation pathway for responsible AI-assisted neuroimaging triage.

13 Conclusion
This article specifies a practical CT–MRI triage
research platform for stroke, hemorrhage, and
mass effect. It integrates modality-aware in-
gestion, preprocessing, feature-based and deep-
learning models, explainability, clinician-readable
output, and governance. The combined mode
permits CT and MRI images to pass through
the complete process in one study while pre-
serving modality identity. The framework is
ready for controlled dataset ingestion and em-
pirical evaluation; it is not a substitute for clini-
cal diagnosis. The next stage is to populate the
train, validation, and test folders with ethically
approved data, execute the prespecified evalu-
ation, and report both performance and failure
modes.

Data and Code Availability
The project prototype, configuration, notebooks,
and application are maintained in the accompa-
nying AI Medical Imaging workspace. Dataset
access is subject to the license and ethics con-
ditions of each source. No patient-identifying
data should be committed to the repository.
